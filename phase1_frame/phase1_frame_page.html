Problem statement

Many drivers, one slot, no false promises

How might SetuHaul let drivers report delays, ask questions and accept revised dock appointments in plain conversation, while simultaneous requests meet limited warehouse capacity, without ever making an infeasible or conflicting promise?

280–360loads per day
32dock doors, 6 facilities
190–240appointments per day
20–35messages in 30 min during a spike
5ops coordinators per shift
0live GPS feeds

This is a high-correctness system under burst contention, not a high-throughput one. Engineering effort goes to concurrency control, idempotency and escalation. The LLM handles messy language at the edges. Feasibility, holds and commits are deterministic code backed by database constraints.

The two partial unique indexes stop two active bookings on the same slot row. They do not stop overlapping slots, spill-over unloads, bookings on blocked or incompatible docks, cancelled shipments with live bookings, a driver in two places, stale holds, or two drivers both being told yes. The Build phase closes these with PostgreSQL exclusion constraints and the transactional booking service.

Problem Definition Sheet

Facts, assumptions and unknowns

Facts

  • 2 facilities, 9 docks, 21 shipments, 106 slots, 20 appointments
  • Jaipur: reefer → D5, > 25 t → heavy dock, no new start after 21:00, 30-min grace
  • Gate processing is 1 min at Jaipur. Gurugram has no check-in history
  • All needed AgentCore features run in Mumbai. Claude there is served via the Global endpoint only

Key assumptions

  • Showing an option reserves nothing. Only an explicit yes creates a short hold. A booking is confirmed only after a re-check and commit
  • A driver's message is evidence. ETAs are fused with independent signals. Safety claims always go to a human
  • Timing parameters are per facility (and per shift), seeded now and learned later
  • Cognito for every human. IAM (SigV4) on every API

Unknowns for the client

  • Warehouse confirmation time per facility and shift (GGN night coordinator is vacant)
  • Gate-to-dock and changeover times: called_to_dock_ts is not captured
  • Are the driver and vehicle double-assignments real or data entry errors?
  • Tracking vendors (ULIP partner, SIM provider) and prices
  • Sign-off on Global-endpoint inference with PII masked
Beyond the driver's word

Independent ETA signals, paid for only when they change a decision

TierTriggerSignalsCost profile
T0Every shipmentPlan, gate check-in, driver chat, existing carrier telematicsNo new spend
T1Within 4 h of the slot, or an exception is openFASTag toll crossings via ULIP or an aggregator (passive, no device)Per lookup, polled every 30 min
T2Declared ETA disagrees by more than 30 min, or LOW confidence on HIGH/CRITICALWhatsApp location-pin request (one tap) plus route timeOne message and one route call
T3HIGH/CRITICAL, reefer or untolled route, and still in doubtSIM-based tracking with trip-scoped consent, stopped at gate-in~₹1–2 per truck-day (vendor claim)
T4Safety claim or contradiction that persistsOps call. Signals are evidence onlyCoordinator time

A deterministic fuse_eta() ranks sources as gate-in, then ops override, fresh telematics, location pin, SIM ping, FASTag, warehouse estimate, driver and plan. It flags impossible speeds, optimistic declarations and breakdowns it can corroborate, and sets confidence from evidence. WhatsApp's Business API delivers single location pins, not live location, so continuous tracking via WhatsApp is not an option. Under DPDP, tracking is trip-scoped and consented, coordinates never reach the LLM, and raw positions are purged after 30 days.

Every human signs in through Cognito: MFA for staff, phone OTP for drivers on the web channel. The identity pool exchanges their token for temporary IAM credentials, with a role per group (ops, planner per facility, dispatcher per carrier, driver). Every API route, AgentCore call and database connection is authorised with IAM. The single exception is the WhatsApp webhook, because Meta cannot sign SigV4: it verifies Meta's HMAC signature, has no other routes, and hands off to IAM-signed calls straight away.

What the spike shows

  • CP-SAT cuts weighted waiting by 32 % against FCFS and breaks the fewest promises
  • "Priority first" is worse than FCFS: docks sit idle while CRITICAL trucks are still on the road
  • The optimum made one NORMAL truck wait 139 min, so a hard maximum yard wait or an ageing term is needed
  • The reefer truck SHP1015 escalates under every method

Decision

  • Per-driver options: is_feasible() plus transparent ranking, under 300 ms
  • Facility re-sequencing: CP-SAT with a 2–5 s limit and a dispatch-rule fallback, in a Python Lambda container
  • OptaPlanner / Timefold not chosen: it needs a JVM service, the official Timefold Python solver was archived in Oct 2025, and its strengths only matter at far larger scale
  • Proposals are applied move by move through the booking service. Moving someone's on-time confirmed slot needs planner approval
OpenStreetMap feasibility

OSM yes, but self-hosted, and precomputed first

OptionVerdictWhy
Public OSM tiles, Nominatim, OSRM demo serverNoDonated capacity. Nominatim allows at most 1 request/s per application. The OSRM demo forbids heavy or SLA use and can be withdrawn
Self-hosted Valhalla on an India OSM extractYesTruck costing (weight, height, axle) applied per request. ODbL attribution. No live traffic, so it is calibrated with our own lane history
Amazon Location ServiceIf in regionManaged and traffic-aware. Availability in ap-south-1 is not yet verified

v1, cost-optimal: no routing server at runtime. Valhalla precomputes, offline, a drive-time matrix from every toll plaza and common pin location to the six facilities, so FASTag plaza → ETA is a table lookup. A routing service on ECS is added only if location-pin volume grows. City no-entry windows for heavy vehicles are kept as our own overlay, because OSM tags them poorly.

Non-functional plan

Performance with Locust, reliability, security

Performance (Locust)

  • SLOs: webhook ack p99 ≤ 1 s, reply p95 ≤ 8 s, options p95 ≤ 300 ms, hold and commit p95 ≤ 200 ms, 0 double bookings
  • Profiles: baseline, spike (35 in 5 min), 10× (350 / 30 min), stress-to-break, 4 h soak, event storm
  • Contention: 10 / 50 / 200 users hit one slot on a barrier, 100 times. Exactly one winner each time
  • A core-only mode with a stubbed model for scale, and full-stack with Bedrock at about $20 per 10× run
  • Locust on ECS Fargate in Mumbai with SigV4-signed users. CI gates on CSV output plus CloudWatch
  • Quota headroom: AgentCore 2,500 sessions vs ~120 needed; Bedrock 2M TPM vs ~0.3M

Reliability

  • If the model fails, the bot falls back to deterministic template replies. Capacity logic never depended on the LLM
  • Multi-AZ Aurora with RDS Proxy. RPO ≤ 5 min, RTO ≤ 30 min. Backups copied to Hyderabad
  • DLQs, idempotent handlers, a reconciler for missed timers, an outbox with channel fallback
  • AWS FIS chaos (Aurora failover during contention, Lambda throttling), Resilience Hub, synthetic canary every 5 min

Security & vulnerabilities

  • OWASP LLM Top 10: identity from the token, never from text. Writes need a shown option plus explicit confirmation, re-validated in code. Grounding post-check. Token caps
  • GuardDuty, Security Hub, Inspector, Config, CloudTrail, WAF, KMS, PrivateLink
  • CI: Bandit, Semgrep, pip-audit, gitleaks, cdk-nag, Checkov, Trivy, ZAP, plus an LLM red-team (promptfoo / garak, Hinglish corpus)
  • Penetration test before go-live. Fix SLAs: critical 7 days, high 30, medium 90

Hard gates (code)

  • D3: no overlapping dock occupancy, ever
  • D4: every time and dock in a reply exists in that turn's tool output
  • D2: every booking passes an independent re-check
  • D10: no PII or coordinates in prompts or logs
  • D6: every escalation has a complete hand-off record

LLM-judge rubric (1–5, anchored)

  • Asks only for missing facts · states status without overpromising · refusal gives a reason and an alternative · brevity for WhatsApp · tone · language match · context retention
  • Pass bar: mean ≥ 4.0, never below 3 on overpromising
  • Judge calibrated on 40 human-labelled transcripts (weighted κ ≥ 0.6)
Human in the loop

People override policy, never integrity

Earlier drafts named when a person steps in but not how. This section adds the workflow. Every human action runs through the same booking service, so a coordinator can approve an after-hours start, but nobody can double-book a dock. Silence is never approval: a request that times out changes nothing and escalates upward.

ModeTriggered byWhoSLA and timeout
ApproveStart after 21:00, moving someone's on-time confirmed slot, dock downgradeFacility plannerExpires with no change. Driver gets the next option
DecideNo feasible slot (SHP1015), contradictory data (DRV006), pending request expired, clarification limit, re-routing or penaltiesOps coordinatorAcknowledge ≤ 10 min (≤ 5 min CRITICAL), then re-route to the shift manager
Take overSafety or accident, distress or abuse, driver asks for a person, repeated agent failureCoordinator; shift manager for safetyImmediate page plus a phone call. The agent goes silent until handback
ConfirmPending warehouse confirmationFacility plannerTTL per facility and shift (GGN night = 0, straight to Decide)
OverseeAlwaysShift manager, ops leadLive board, 10 % sampled review, post-hoc override, per-facility pause switch

What the person gets

  • A complete hand-off record: ETA evidence, constraints, every option tried and why it failed, suggested actions, what the driver was told
  • One-click structured actions that are re-validated by is_feasible()
  • Takeover and handback with a summary, so the assistant never re-asks

Workload and learning

  • With ≥ 70 % automated, about 5–8 human cases on a normal day and 1–2 per coordinator in a spike
  • An alert at more than 4 open cases per coordinator
  • Human decisions become eval scenarios. Frequently approved triggers can become automatic rules, but only through review and versioning
